Students

I welcome UCM graduate students interested in security, networked systems and applied machine learning. You do not need research experience to start; Python and curiosity are enough.

Your first semester, step by step. Each thesis starts as a small production pipeline, agreed at the proposal stage:

  1. Pick one of the open projects and read two papers from its list.
  2. Reproduce a baseline on a public dataset, using the project's "Start here" plan.
  3. Run your first experiment on the project's research question, and attack your own result.
  4. Write it up with the LaTeX and Overleaf guide for a target venue.

Track record. I have supervised six completed M.Sc. theses. Four became journal articles with the student as lead author:

Applied Soft ComputingSensorsJ. Engineering Sciences and DesignExpert Systems

Three more student-led papers are under submission.

To start, email me your CV, the project you picked and the research question you would begin with.

Employers and recruiters

Students in my group practice what security, machine learning and data teams ask for:

  • Building reproducible ML pipelines on real, messy data.
  • Evaluating honestly: fair baselines, leakage checks, attacking their own models.
  • Writing and presenting work to a publication standard.

My courses in machine learning, deep learning, AI, algorithms and databases run on interactive notes where students write and run code every week. Employers looking for students with these skills are welcome to write.

Collaborators and industry

My driver-identification work with General Motors Research led to a U.S. patent. I am glad to talk about vehicle and IoT security data, intrusion detection in real deployments, quantum machine learning benchmarks, and student capstone projects.
