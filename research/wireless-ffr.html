Sharing spectrum fairly: fractional frequency reuse

Before security, I worked on how cellular networks share spectrum. My M.S. thesis at Bilkent asked how to split a cell's subcarriers between users near the base station and users at the edge, so the network is both fast and fair.

The trade-off

Users near a base station have strong signals and can share the whole band with neighboring cells. Users at the cell edge hear the neighbors' interference too, so in fractional frequency reuse they are served on parts of the band that adjacent cells do not use at the same time. Every subcarrier given to the inner region raises total throughput but leaves less for edge users, so fairness falls.

The thesis measured both sides, cell throughput and Jain's fairness index, and used their product as one score for the trade-off. It compared static splits with adaptive ones that follow where users actually are.

  1. Place usersinner region or cell edge
  2. Split subcarriersinner share versus edge share
  3. Scheduleserve each region on its part of the band
  4. Measurecell throughput and Jain's fairness
  5. Adaptmove the split as users move
≈5%higher throughput × fairness from adapting the split to the user distribution, over the best fixed split
