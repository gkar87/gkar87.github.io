LaTeX and Overleaf for your first paper

A practical guide for my students: start from the official IEEE or ACM template, organize the project, cite with BibTeX, make clean figures and tables, write together on Overleaf, and check everything before you submit.

Why LaTeX, and why Overleaf

Almost every computer science and engineering venue we submit to, IEEE and ACM conferences and journals alike, provides an official LaTeX template, and reviewers expect papers to look like it. LaTeX keeps numbering of sections, figures, tables, equations and references automatic, so you can move things around without breaking anything. Overleaf runs LaTeX in the browser: nothing to install, one shared copy of the paper, comments and history built in. In my group every paper lives in one Overleaf project shared with me from day one.

You do not need to know LaTeX before you start. Plan one afternoon for the basics below; you will learn the rest while writing.

Your first afternoon

  1. Step 1Create a free Overleaf account with your university email and read Your first project.
  2. Step 2Work through Learn LaTeX in 30 minutes, typing the examples rather than copying them.
  3. Step 3Keep The Not So Short Introduction to LaTeX (on CTAN) open as a reference, and use Overleaf's tutorial list when you want more.
  4. Step 4When something will not compile, read Fixing LaTeX errors and fix the first error in the log first; later errors are often caused by it.

Start from the official template

Never start a paper from a blank file or from someone else's paper. Start from the venue's official template, and check the call for papers for the exact version, page limit and whether the review is anonymous.

IEEE conferences

IEEE's manuscript templates page links the LaTeX template and its instructions, and IEEE's own templates are in the Overleaf IEEE gallery. The class is IEEEtran; use its conference option. IEEE warns that guidance text left over from the template can stop a paper from being published, so delete all of it.

ACM conferences and journals

ACM's Primary Article Template page describes the acmart class; most proceedings use its sigconf format. The official versions are in the Overleaf ACM gallery. For double-blind review, add the options the call for papers asks for, usually review and anonymous.

% IEEE conference paper: the first lines of main.tex
\documentclass[conference]{IEEEtran}
\usepackage{graphicx}   % figures
\usepackage{booktabs}   % clean tables
\usepackage{amsmath}    % equations
\usepackage{cite}       % sorted, compressed citations [1]-[4]

% ACM instead:  \documentclass[sigconf,review,anonymous]{acmart}

Overleaf explains creating a project from a template, and you can upload a project as a zip when a venue provides its own files.

Organize the project

One file per section keeps edits small and makes it easy for two people to work at once.

main.tex            % preamble, title, \input{...} for each section, bibliography
sections/
  introduction.tex
  related.tex
  method.tex
  evaluation.tex
  conclusion.tex
figures/            % PDF figures exported from your plotting scripts
refs.bib            % every reference, one BibTeX entry each
  • Pull each section in with \input{sections/method}.
  • Keep the scripts that make figures and tables in your Git repository, not in Overleaf; Overleaf holds the paper.
  • Write one sentence per line. Changes, comments and history become much easier to read.
  • Label everything with a prefix, such as fig:, tab:, sec: and eq:, and refer to it with \ref, never by typing a number.

Citations with BibTeX

Overleaf's guide to creating a bibliography covers the three steps: a .bib file, a bibliography style, and \cite commands in the text. See also working with .bib files and the available styles. For IEEE papers use the IEEEtran style that comes with the template; acmart sets its own.

@inproceedings{kar2014jammer,
  author    = {Kar, Gorkem and Mustafa, Hossen and Wang, Yan and Chen, Yingying
               and Xu, Wenyuan and Gruteser, Marco and Vu, Tam},
  title     = {Detection of On-Road Vehicles Emanating {GPS} Interference},
  booktitle = {Proceedings of the ACM Conference on Computer and Communications Security (CCS)},
  year      = {2014},
  doi       = {10.1145/2660267.2660336}
}

As shown in prior work~\cite{kar2014jammer}, ...
\bibliographystyle{IEEEtran}
\bibliography{refs}
  • Copy BibTeX from the publisher's page (IEEE Xplore, the ACM Digital Library) or from the DOI, then check every field. Search engines often get authors, venues and capitalization wrong.
  • Protect capitals that must stay capitals with braces, as in {GPS} and {IoT}.
  • Cite only what you have read, and cite the published version rather than a preprint when one exists.
  • If you keep references in Zotero, Overleaf can link to it on premium plans; otherwise export a .bib file and upload it.

Figures and tables

Start with Overleaf's pages on inserting images and tables, then use booktabs for tables that look professional.

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/detection-delay.pdf}
  \caption{Detection delay against false alarms per hour for three attack speeds.}
  \label{fig:delay}
\end{figure}

\begin{table}[t]
  \centering
  \caption{Accuracy under random and time-based splits.}
  \label{tab:splits}
  \begin{tabular}{lrr}
    \toprule
    Model & Random split & Time split \\
    \midrule
    k-NN  & --   & --   \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}
  • Export plots as PDF so they stay sharp at any zoom, and set font sizes in the plotting script so the text in a figure matches the paper.
  • Make figures readable in grayscale and for color-blind readers: vary line style and marker as well as color.
  • Put \label after \caption, or the reference will point to the wrong number.
  • A caption should say what the reader should notice, not only what is plotted.
  • Table captions go above the table and figure captions below, as the IEEE and ACM templates do.

Writing together

  • Sharing: share the project with me by email invitation, not with a public link. Projects are private by default; keep them that way until the paper is out.
  • Comments: use comments for questions and resolve them when they are settled.
  • Track changes: track changes shows every edit for review; it needs a premium plan on the project owner's account, so check what your university provides.
  • History: history and versioning lets you label the version you submitted and go back to it.
  • Git: Overleaf can sync with Git and GitHub on premium plans, which keeps the paper next to its code.

Before you submit

  1. Page limit met, counting exactly what the call for papers counts (references often do not count; appendices often do).
  2. For double-blind review: no author names, no acknowledgments, no links to your own repositories, and your own earlier work cited in the third person.
  3. All template guidance text removed.
  4. No warnings about undefined references or citations, and no ?? anywhere in the PDF.
  5. Every figure and table referenced in the text, in order, and readable when printed.
  6. Every number in the text matches the tables, and every table matches the scripts that produced it.
  7. References complete: authors, title, venue, year, pages or DOI, with capitalization protected.
  8. Spell check run, and the abstract read aloud once.
  9. If the venue asks you to check the PDF with a publisher tool, do it before the deadline day.
  10. For a preprint, work through Overleaf's arXiv checklist, and read Overleaf's notes on submitting to publishers.
  11. Send me the PDF at least one week before the deadline. Nothing is submitted without every author's approval.

How we write papers in my group

  1. Week 1Write the title, a five-sentence abstract and the list of figures before any prose. If the figures do not tell the story, the text will not either.
  2. Week 2Fill in method and evaluation, the sections you know best. Introduction and related work come last.
  3. WeeklyEach Friday, leave a short comment at the top of main.tex saying what changed and what you are stuck on.
  4. Before reviewLabel the version in Overleaf history, export the PDF and send it for internal review.
