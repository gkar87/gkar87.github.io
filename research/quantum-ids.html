Hybrid quantum-classical intrusion detection

Can a small quantum circuit, paired with classical deep learning, help catch network attacks and mobile malware? We built one framework that does both.

The question

Can a small quantum circuit inside a deep-learning pipeline help catch network attacks and mobile malware?

Why it matters

A quantum layer has very few parameters, which matters for detectors that must run on gateways and phones.

What we found

In simulation, one hybrid framework detected DDoS traffic with 99.86% accuracy and classified 12 Android malware families with 95.33%, against 86.80% and 78.94% for classical CNNs.

One framework for DDoS and Android malware

Intrusion detectors for software-defined networks and malware classifiers for phones are usually separate systems. We designed a single hybrid quantum-classical neural network (HQCNN) that handles both: binary detection of DDoS traffic, and multiclass classification of Android malware families. Signals are turned into images with wavelets, a convolutional network extracts features, and a compact quantum layer makes the final decision.

  1. Normalizemin–max scaling
  2. Wavelet transformdiscrete wavelet denoising
  3. Scaleogramcontinuous wavelet → image
  4. 2D CNNfeature extraction
  5. LDAreduce to n−1 features
  6. Quantum layer2-qubit variational circuit
  7. Decisionclassical output layer

These results come from quantum simulators. The appeal for security is size: the quantum layer has very few parameters, which matters for detectors that must run on IoT gateways and phones.

The same idea in spectroscopy

The wavelet-to-quantum pipeline also works on light. In IEEE Access (2025) we turned Raman spectra of human serum into wavelet scaleograms and classified COVID-19 with a quanvolutional neural network, comparing it with classical CNNs. In AVS Quantum Science (2026), a classical-quantum network with dual-channel feature extraction ran on an IonQ quantum processor to classify weathered microplastics from their Raman spectra. The spectroscopy page has more, with a live quanvolution demo.

Where this is going

Moving hybrid and compact deep models from offline benchmarks to deployable detectors for IoT gateways and mobile devices, with explanations a network operator can act on.

Discussion

What it shows

A very small quantum circuit can sit inside a classical deep-learning pipeline and reach high accuracy on two different security tasks, DDoS detection and Android malware classification.

Where it falls short

  • These results come from quantum simulators, which have no hardware noise and no cost per run.
  • Public benchmark datasets can contain shortcuts that inflate accuracy for any model, quantum or not.
  • Showing that the quantum layer itself is responsible needs classical models of the same size and tuning budget.

What it means

A quantum advantage is a hypothesis to test, not a result to assume. That is exactly what the honest quantum benchmarks project sets out to do.

Open research questions

Questions this work leaves open. They are directions, not results; each links to the planned project that takes it up.

  1. Where, if anywhere, does a quantum layer beat a classical model of the same size and tuning budget?

    Why it is open
    The case for hybrid models today is comparable accuracy at a very small parameter budget. A claim of advantage needs matched classical baselines and statistics.
    Taken up in
    honest quantum benchmarks

  2. Can compact hybrid detectors run on IoT gateways and phones, with explanations an operator can act on?

    Why it is open
    Offline benchmarks do not show whether a detector fits a gateway's budget or whether its alerts can be trusted.
    Taken up in
    on-device detection · checkable explanations

  3. Does the robustness of hybrid models hold up against attackers who must keep traffic valid?

    Why it is open
    Our hybrid quantum work measured a robustness advantage against adversarial perturbation. Whether it survives realistic, problem-space attacks is open.
    Taken up in
    realistic evasion attacks

  4. Can intrusion detectors be trained across many sites without pooling their traffic?

    Why it is open
    Gateways see different traffic and often cannot share it. Federated training keeps data local, but it opens a new door to poisoned updates.
    Taken up in
    federated intrusion detection

Start here

A first month for a student joining this work, in 3 steps. Do them in order: each one uses what the one before produced.

Goal for the month: A fair classical baseline for our quantum intrusion detection results, run on the same data with the same budget.

Before you start: setup for every project
  1. Set up a workspace. Create a private Git repository for the project with folders for data/, src/, figures/ and notes/. Make a Python virtual environment (python -m venv .venv) and record every package you install in requirements.txt.
  2. Keep a lab notebook. Add a dated entry to notes/log.md each time you work: what you ran (the exact command), what came out, and what surprised you. This log is the first thing to share when you ask for help, and most of your paper's method section comes from it.
  3. Make every number reproducible. Fix random seeds, write down the dataset version and how you split it, and keep the script that produced each figure next to the figure. If you cannot regenerate a number with one command, it is not finished.
  4. Stay legal and ethical. Work only with public datasets, data you recorded yourself with everyone's consent, or systems you own. Never test an attack on a network, car, radio band or service that is not yours, and follow the safety note on this page.
  5. When you get stuck. Spend about an hour, then write down what you tried, what you expected and the exact error or plot, and ask. A precise question is much easier to answer than "it does not work".
  1. Step 1 · Week 1Read our paper as a reviewer
    • Read our EPJ Quantum Technology paper.
    • Write down every dataset, preprocessing step, model size (number of trainable parameters) and metric it reports.

    You are done when you have a table someone could use to rebuild the experiments.

  2. Step 2 · Week 2Learn the quantum tools
    • Work through PennyLane's variational classifier tutorials until you can train one on a toy dataset.
    • Count its trainable parameters and time one training run.

    You are done when your notebook trains a variational classifier and prints its parameter count.

  3. Step 3 · Weeks 3 and 4Build the baseline every claim must beat
    • Take CICIoT2023 or CIC-AndMal2020 with the paper's preprocessing.
    • Train a classical model (small neural network or tree model) with about the same number of parameters as the quantum model.
    • Run both over several random splits and report the mean and spread, not one number.

    You are done when you have one table: quantum vs classical, same data, same parameter budget, several runs.
