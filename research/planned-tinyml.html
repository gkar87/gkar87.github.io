Intrusion detection that fits on the device

Most IoT devices cannot run a full intrusion detector. This project shrinks detectors until they run on a microcontroller, and measures what accuracy that costs.

Why this matters

My group identifies IoT devices and attacks from packet headers alone, which keeps the input small. Today such detectors run on a server or gateway. Running them on the device itself, or on a cheap board beside it, would catch attacks even when the gateway is bypassed and would keep traffic data local. TinyML techniques such as pruning and 8-bit quantization now fit useful models into a few hundred kilobytes, but security detectors have their own needs: few false alarms, and robustness to traffic the model has not seen.

Research questions

  1. How small can a packet-header detector get, through feature selection, pruning and quantization, before detection accuracy or false-alarm rate degrades?

    Hypothesis
    Header-level detectors keep their detection rate down to a few kilobytes, and quantization costs less than pruning.
    How we would test it
    Compress step by step and plot detection rate and false alarms against model size on fixed test sets.

  2. Which model families (small trees, tiny neural networks, autoencoders) give the best accuracy per kilobyte and per millijoule?

    Hypothesis
    Small trees give the best accuracy per kilobyte on header features; tiny networks win only when timing patterns matter.
    How we would test it
    Compare families at matched memory budgets on a microcontroller, measuring energy per inference.

  3. Can an on-device detector keep learning normal traffic without being taught to accept an attack?

    Hypothesis
    Unguarded online updates can be poisoned by a slow attack; gating updates on confidence prevents most of it.
    How we would test it
    Simulate slow poisoning during online learning and compare gated and ungated update rules.

Expected contributions

  • Detectors compressed to microcontroller size, with code.
  • Accuracy per kilobyte and per millijoule across model families.
  • An update rule that keeps learning without being poisoned.

Approach

  1. BaselineTrain detectors on public IoT attack datasets with our feature selection pipeline.
  2. CompressPrune, quantize to 8 bits and distill into small models.
  3. DeployRun them on boards such as an ESP32 or an Arm Cortex-M with TensorFlow Lite Micro.
  4. MeasureAccuracy, false alarms, memory, latency and energy on the board.
  5. ReportAn accuracy-per-kilobyte table that others can reproduce.

Discussion

If it works

Even small networks get intrusion detection that runs on the devices themselves.

Where it could fail

  • Compression can hide a drop in accuracy on rare attacks, which are often the ones that matter.
  • Microcontrollers differ widely, so results on one board may not transfer.

How we will know

Accuracy for each attack class, plus memory, energy and latency on real microcontrollers.

Start here

A concrete first month for a student joining this work. Each step builds on the one before.

  1. Step 1Read the papers below.
  2. Step 2Train a small detector on TON_IoT network data in PyTorch and count its weights.
  3. Step 3Quantize it to 8 bits and deploy it to a microcontroller board with Edge Impulse, then measure accuracy and memory.

Papers to read first

  1. Kitsune: an ensemble of autoencoders for online network intrusion detection
    Y. Mirsky, T. Doitshman, Y. Elovici, A. Shabtai
    NDSS, 2018
    A lightweight online detector designed to run near the network edge.
    Read
  2. N-BaIoT: Network-based detection of IoT botnet attacks using deep autoencoders
    Y. Meidan, M. Bohadana, Y. Mathov, Y. Mirsky, A. Shabtai, D. Breitenbacher, Y. Elovici
    IEEE Pervasive Computing, 2018
    A widely used IoT botnet dataset to start from.
    Read
  3. Deep compression: compressing deep neural networks with pruning, trained quantization and Huffman coding
    S. Han, H. Mao, W. J. Dally
    ICLR, 2016
    The classic recipe for making models small.
    Read
  4. Quantization and training of neural networks for efficient integer-arithmetic-only inference
    B. Jacob, S. Kligys, B. Chen, M. Zhu, M. Tang, A. Howard, H. Adam, D. Kalenichenko
    IEEE/CVF CVPR, 2018
    The 8-bit integer approach behind most microcontroller deployments.
    Read
  5. MCUNet: tiny deep learning on IoT devices
    J. Lin, W.-M. Chen, Y. Lin, J. Cohn, C. Gan, S. Han
    NeurIPS, 2020
    Shows how far careful design can push models on microcontrollers.
    Read
  6. MLPerf Tiny benchmark
    C. Banbury, V. J. Reddi, P. Torelli, et al.
    arXiv preprint, 2021
    How to measure accuracy, latency and energy fairly on tiny devices.
    Read
  7. Comparative analysis of feature selection algorithms for automated IoT device fingerprinting
    A. Aksoy, S. Varma, G. Moorthy, E. Pan, G. Kar
    ICISSP, 2024
    Our header-only features, a natural fit for tiny models.
    Read
