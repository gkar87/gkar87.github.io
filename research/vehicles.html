Connected vehicles: sensing, identity and trust

Cars are rolling sensor platforms. This line of work asks what their data can reveal, who is driving, and how to catch the signals that put everyone on the road at risk.

Finding the car with the GPS jammer

Cheap personal GPS jammers, plugged into a car's lighter socket, knock out GPS for everyone nearby, including aircraft and emergency systems. Locating the moving source is hard because the signal is strong only for a few seconds. We built a system that combines roadside monitoring points (a software-defined radio, a directional antenna and a camera) with ordinary smartphones, which already report GPS signal strength.

The key idea: cars drive at different speeds, so the group of cars near the jammer keeps changing. One monitoring point leaves a few suspects; a second point a few miles on leaves only the one car that was near the peak both times.

200 hof passive GPS-band monitoring at five sites in New Jersey and South Carolina

Who is driving? PredriveID and a GM patent

Before a car even moves, each driver performs the same small ritual in their own rhythm: close the door, start the engine, shift out of park, buckle up, release the brake. Working with General Motors Research, we showed that the time gaps between these events, read from the car's own data bus, identify the driver within seconds, with no camera and nothing the driver has to carry.

This work led to U.S. Patent No. 10,332,020, Method and apparatus of differentiating drivers based on driving behaviors (GM Global Technology Operations and Rutgers, 2019).

Traffic from dash cams at the vehicle edge

Dash-cam video from ordinary cars can estimate traffic in real time: we detect and count the vehicles in view and estimate their speed and lane on the vehicle itself, so the estimate arrives while it is still useful.

<10%speed estimation error across roads and traffic conditions
>91%lane estimation accuracy, left-most lane
>90%vehicle counting accuracy even at peak hours, left-most lane

Data: Kar et al., ACM/IEEE SEC 2017 (Real-time traffic estimation at vehicular edge nodes), abstract.

Distracted driving from cameras and car sensors

With my M.Sc. student Furkan Omerustaoglu, we detected nine distracted-driving behaviors with a two-stage model: convolutional networks read the driver-facing camera, and LSTM networks combine those image features with in-vehicle sensor data. Adding the sensor data significantly improved detection over images alone.

Where this is going

Connected vehicles now trust data from roadside units, other cars and the cloud, and all three can be spoofed. The next step is detecting spoofed, jammed or manipulated sensor and V2X data in real time on the vehicle and roadside hardware itself, where latency and memory are tight.

Open research questions

Questions this work leaves open. They are directions, not results; each links to the planned project that takes it up.

  1. Can a vehicle catch spoofed, jammed or manipulated sensor and V2X data in real time, on hardware where latency and memory are tight?

    Why it is open
    Cars now trust data from roadside units, other vehicles and the cloud, and every one of those channels can be spoofed.
    Taken up in
    GNSS spoofing · ghost vehicles in V2X · in-vehicle network

  2. Can behavioral signatures in raw sensor streams, like the pre-trip rhythm that identifies a driver, serve as trust anchors for the data a car relies on?

    Why it is open
    PredriveID showed that bus signals carry a stable signature of who is driving. Whether the same kind of signature can vouch for the data itself is open.
    Taken up in
    GNSS spoofing · in-vehicle network

  3. How can cars share location data for traffic services without revealing where their drivers live and work?

    Why it is open
    My dissertation addressed data availability and privacy for connected cars. Location traces remain one of the most identifying kinds of data a car produces.
    Taken up in
    vehicle location privacy · EV charging security

Start here

A concrete first month for a student joining this work. Each step builds on the one before.

  1. Step 1Read the GPS-jammer and PredriveID papers below, and write one paragraph on what each assumed about the attacker.
  2. Step 2Download the one-minute comma2k19 sample and plot GNSS speed against wheel speed for the same drive.
  3. Step 3Build a short highway scenario in SUMO and log every vehicle's position, the raw material for roadside detection.
  4. Step 4Pick one open question below and write a one-page plan: data, method and how you would measure success.
