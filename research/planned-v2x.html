Catching ghost vehicles in V2X messages

Connected cars broadcast their position and speed many times a second, and other cars act on those messages. This project detects messages that describe cars that are not there.

Why this matters

Vehicle-to-everything (V2X) messages are signed, so a receiver can tell that a message came from a certified sender. A signature cannot tell whether the message is true. A compromised unit, or an insider with valid credentials, can report a ghost car braking hard ahead and trigger emergency braking behind it. Detecting that kind of misbehavior has to look at what the messages say and check it against physics and against independent sensors.

My research statement's first direction is exactly this: detecting spoofed, jammed or manipulated sensor and V2X data on vehicle and roadside hardware. It builds on our roadside detection of GPS jammers (ACM CCS 2014) and on traffic estimation at vehicular edge nodes (ACM/IEEE SEC 2017).

Research questions

  1. Which plausibility checks on position, speed and heading catch fake vehicles in V2X messages, and how do they trade detection against false alarms?

    Hypothesis
    Simple physical checks catch crude ghosts; attackers who obey physics can only be caught with evidence from independent sensors.
    How we would test it
    Evaluate checks on public misbehavior datasets and on simulated attacks of rising sophistication.

  2. How much do roadside cameras, radar and other cars' reports add, and where are the coverage gaps an attacker can use?

    Hypothesis
    Fusing independent sensors closes most gaps, and the remaining risk sits where no independent sensor looks.
    How we would test it
    Simulate traffic with roadside and on-board sensor coverage, place attacks inside and outside it, and measure detection.

  3. Can misbehavior detection run within the latency and memory budget of roadside and on-board units?

    Hypothesis
    Single-message checks fit easily on board; fusing several sensors is the costly step and belongs on roadside edge computers.
    How we would test it
    Profile each detector on embedded boards at realistic message rates.

Expected contributions

  • A layered ghost-vehicle detector combining plausibility checks with roadside and on-board sensors.
  • A coverage analysis showing where attacks can still hide.
  • Run-time profiles on roadside and on-board hardware.

Approach

  1. Start from dataUse public misbehavior datasets with labeled attacks.
  2. SimulateAdd ghost vehicles of rising sophistication in a traffic and network simulator.
  3. Check plausibilityTest each message against physics and the sender's own history.
  4. Fuse sensorsCross-check against simulated roadside cameras and on-board radar.
  5. DeployProfile the detectors on embedded boards at realistic message rates.

All attacks run in simulation or on recorded data. Nothing is broadcast on real V2X channels.
